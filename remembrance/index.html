Remembrance Day · November 11

Lest We Forget

A short film for the ones who stood at Vimy Ridge, at Dunkirk, and at Juno Beach — and for the ones who never came home.

The words of the film

He was nineteen. A private on a gun crew, writing a letter home to his girlfriend, in the mud below Vimy Ridge, feeding the guns.

He was not a general. None of them were. They were privates and corporals, gunners and kids from farms and fishing towns.

From the ridge to the beaches, they kept standing. At Dunkirk, British and French soldiers were trapped against the sea, and the boats came for them — warships and little ships, Canadian sailors among the rescuers — and carried them home. At Normandy, our own boys stormed the sand at Juno Beach and kept going.

And those who fell gave their lives so that we could live in freedom, in a country at peace. Everything we have, they paid for — our homes, our families, our quiet mornings. All they ask is that we remember.

So remember the guns. Remember the ridge. Remember them.

Lest we forget.
