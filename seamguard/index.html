SeamGuard

The guard that fuses the seams between your agents and brains

Your agents hand work to each other all day. SeamGuard checks every hand-off — who is passing it, and whether it is allowed to — before the work moves.

And for the doors themselves: Amos secures your doors — and the identity check on your money.

The problem

Brains leak at the joins

Your doors are not the only place agents meet. Inside your own system, your agents hand work to each other all day — and the joins between them are where things leak.

Context bleeds across from one job to the next. A figure one agent invented arrives at the next desk as fact. Two agents disagree, and the disagreement gets quietly settled by whichever one spoke last. Work gets done twice — or done, undone, and done again.

Nobody forged a key. Nobody broke a lock. The work simply crossed from one agent to another with nobody checking the crossing.

The fix

Every hand-off becomes a checked crossing

SeamGuard puts a guard at those seams. Every hand-off between your agents becomes a checked crossing, with two keys that both have to turn:

Key one — who is knocking

SeamGuard checks the identity: an issued key, a signature that verifies, and a signed receipt written for the crossing. An agent that cannot prove who it is does not get through — an unknown identity bounces.

Key two — what it is allowed to do

The permission check: the crossing has to sit inside that agent’s mandate — what its owner said it may ask, send, and set in motion. Outside the mandate, the crossing stops.

Both checks must say yes. One yes is not enough, and there is no anonymous crossing anywhere.

How it runs

A crossing, step by step

  1. An agent knocks. It presents its issued key and signs the hand-off.
  2. SeamGuard checks the identity. The signature must verify against the key on record. If it cannot prove who it is, the crossing bounces — nothing passes.
  3. SeamGuard checks the mandate. The ask has to sit inside what that agent is allowed to do. Outside the mandate, the crossing stops.
  4. The crossing is written down. A signed receipt records who crossed, what crossed, and when — a record neither side can quietly rewrite afterwards.
  5. The work moves — once. Checked, receipted, and done a single time.

And if the two checks ever disagree, the crossing stops and goes up to a human. It never settles itself, and no agent decides a dispute it is part of.

At your outside doors, nothing loosens: anything crossing in or out of your house gets the full checks, every time. The checks are kept cheap and local on purpose — a guard that slows the work down is a guard people switch off.

Who it serves

For anyone running more than one agent

If a single agent does all your work alone, you may not need SeamGuard yet. The moment two or more agents hand work to each other — a researcher passing to a writer, a planner passing to a builder, a desk passing to an executor — the joins appear, and so do the leaks.

The payoff is plain: the work gets done once. No leaks to chase down, no re-renders, no rework — the guard pays for itself in the work you never have to do twice.
