Core

  • The stranger who was not a stranger — Agents now buy, sell and settle payments while their owners sleep — and nobody on the other side can prove who they are dealing with. Amos answers that one question at the door. (Amos)
  • The leak was in the join — Your agents hand work to each other all day, and the joins between them are where things quietly go wrong. SeamGuard puts a guard at every seam — and two keys have to turn before work crosses. (SeamGuard)
  • The school the wires never reached — Some schools have no internet, no library and no way to reach either. School in a Box puts a whole school on one small computer — and carries it to them as a gift. (School in a Box)

Articles · Money & Agents

Money & Agents

  • A company card in a machine’s hand — Companies want agents that buy, book and pay — but an agent with a card is a risk at machine speed. Agent Spending Power lets an agent spend freely inside a mandate you set, and nowhere outside it. (Agent Spending Power)
  • The invoice that looked exactly right — One look-alike invoice, with new bank details, can take a small business down. Verified Payment Requests let a customer prove a request really came from you — before any money moves. (Verified Payment Requests)
  • Show me exactly what happened — When an AI gets it wrong, the first question is always the same — and most businesses cannot answer it. The AI Receipt Book writes a signed receipt for every action, at the moment it happens. (AI Receipt Book)
  • Which agent did this — Agents share API keys and borrow human logins, so nobody can answer the simplest question in the building. The Company Agent Identity Gate gives every agent its own key, in its own name. (Company Agent Identity Gate)
  • Your own agent, at three in the morning — Identity stops intruders. It does not stop a properly identified agent that starts behaving badly. Behaviour Watch reads actions against the mandate as they happen — and can stop an agent the moment it steps outside. (Behaviour Watch + Kill Switch)
  • The goods are gone, then the money goes too — Online sellers ship the goods, then watch the payment get clawed back. Chargeback Evidence Packs build the proof as the sale happens — so a dispute is fought with a signed chain, not paperwork. (Chargeback Evidence Packs)
  • Let the good agents in — Almost a third of internet traffic is bad bots — but blocking everything automated now blocks your own customers too. The Good Agents Front Door welcomes the agents who can prove who they are, and challenges the rest. (Good Agents Front Door)
  • The key that never dies — Machine-to-machine access still runs on shared secret keys that work forever once they leak. Caller Identity for APIs gives every calling system an identity of its own instead. (Caller Identity for APIs)
  • A broadcast is not a proof — A drone’s broadcast ID says who it claims to be, and anyone with a transmitter can make the same claim. Fleet Identity gives every unit an identity it can prove. (Fleet Identity)
  • Strangers hiring strangers — A client fears paying for work that never comes, and a freelancer fears delivering work that is never paid for. Verified Escrow checks both sides first, and holds the release to the rules. (Verified Escrow)
  • The card on the counter — Clinics, gyms, and repair shops still run on plastic cards, phone numbers, and knowing a face. Customer ID Card gives a local business a card it can actually check, and cancel. (Customer ID Card)

Articles · Health & Care

Health & Care

  • The ward full of strangers — Pumps, monitors, and imaging devices cross onto hospital networks all day, and at most doors nobody checks who they are. WardDoor gives every device a name it has to prove. (WardDoor)
  • The reading that travelled alone — Care at home runs on readings that cross a home network, a phone, and a vendor cloud before anyone sees them. HomeWard proves which device sent each one. (HomeWard)
  • When the pendant calls — Homes are filling with pendants, dispensers, and alarms that send alerts into a person’s house and out of it. SteadyHome makes sure an alert provably came from her own device. (SteadyHome)
  • One login for the crown-jewel machine — A single surgical platform can serve a whole operating program, yet its service door still runs on shared technician credentials. SteadyHand gives every technician an identity of their own. (SteadyHand)
  • The toy that answers to a parent — Connected toys carry children’s voices, images, and locations to clouds by default. PlayCheck encodes the parent’s consent as the rule every crossing is checked against. (PlayCheck)

Articles · Industry & Machines

Industry & Machines

  • Who touched this machine — From January 2027, European machinery law asks who connected to a machine, what they changed, and where the evidence is. MachineBook is the record that answers. (MachineBook)
  • Who told this cell to do that? — On a factory line, instructions cross between machines all day and nobody can prove where they came from. LineSeam puts two keys at every joint and leaves the answer in writing. (LineSeam)
  • Five makers in one warehouse — Most warehouses run robots from different makers through one system, with no common check between them. AisleCross gives every machine one identity and one door they all answer to. (AisleCross)
  • The record the farmer can hold — Dealers and clouds cross into farm machines all season, and the farmer holds no record of any of it. FieldDoor checks every machine before it works and puts the record in the farmer’s hands. (FieldDoor)

Articles · Infrastructure & Cities

Infrastructure & Cities

  • A door worthy of the water supply — Inspectors keep finding the same open door at water systems: shared logins and passwords never changed. TapCheck gives every person and vendor a key of their own, and receipts every entry. (TapCheck)
  • Your building obeys anyone who asks — Most building systems take orders without asking who is speaking. TowerCheck makes sure the orders come from someone allowed to give them, and receipts every vendor session. (TowerCheck)
  • The depot where every name is known — Transit systems still let vendors and staff cross on shared accounts. DepotCheck replaces them with issued identity, scopes every session to its job, and keeps receipts an agency can stand behind. (DepotCheck)
  • The modem that learned to say no — In the KA-SAT attack, no terminal could question the instruction that disabled it. GroundDoor checks every command before it crosses to space, so an unprovable order does not fly. (GroundDoor)
  • Give yours a name at birth — Devices with no identity become other people’s armies. PlugCheck gives every connected product a birth identity, checks its updates, and writes the evidence file as it goes. (PlugCheck)
  • The attacks arrive at the crossings — Connected vehicles are reached remotely, through telematics, clouds and service tools. FleetDoor checks every arrival, and keeps a signed record across the vehicle’s whole life. (FleetDoor)
