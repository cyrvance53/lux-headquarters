FleetDoor

The attacks arrive at the crossings, not at the car. FleetDoor checks every arrival.

The fear is real

494 reported incidents in a single year — 92% of them remote

Upstream Security's 2026 report counted 494 publicly reported automotive cybersecurity incidents in 2025, up 21% year over year — 92% carried out remotely, and 67% involving telematics or cloud vectors (vendor research). The crossings are where the danger lives.

The relief

A vehicle's whole life, on a signed record

Vehicles, dealer tools, and cloud services each hold an issued identity. What a service session may do is mandate-checked, and signed receipts follow the vehicle from build to sale to service to resale — the audit trail UNECE R155 expects.

The product

How FleetDoor works

  • Identity for vehicles, tools, and clouds. Every party at the crossing, known.
  • Service sessions, mandate-checked. A dealer tool may do its job — and nothing past it.
  • Receipts across the vehicle's life. Build, sale, service, resale — one signed story.
  • Revoke one tool or vehicle. Precisely that one, the moment you say so.

One engine runs under every door on this shelf: Amos checks who is knocking. SeamGuard checks the joins between systems. And every check leaves a signed receipt.
